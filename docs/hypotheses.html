1 · H54-A — State-map under-map complement LEGACY; STRICT LANE FAIL

LayersUSGS State Geologic Map Compilation fault layers for Nevada and California (NV.zip, CA.zip, public domain) rasterised to the competition grid; the organizer catalogue (data/grid/labels.tif) used only as an exclusion mask.
Physical signatureIndependent-source divergence, then geometry. This legacy candidate looked for line-like components present in an external geologic compilation but absent from the cached catalogue mask. The geometric filters are an orientation-invariant principal-axis eigenvalue ratio (λ₁/λ₂ ≥ 6) and a minimum principal extent of 5 cells. These are heuristic raster-shape gates, not evidence of age or fault activity.
Why it might catch catalogue-missing faultsA feature present in an independently compiled map but absent from the local catalogue could be a candidate omission. It could equally reflect differences in map scale, age, generalization, or class definitions; the official new-label definition was not re-verified here. Local counts (60,988 catalogue cells, 82,151 SGMC cells, 61,664 SGMC cells more than 300 m from the catalogue) describe cached rasters only.
How it differs from prior workPrior work treated SGMC as one input among many and pruned within 200 m of the catalogue. This candidate (a) excludes the full 300 m kernel, so that no emitted dot can be earning credit from a catalogued fault; (b) replaces bounding-box elongation — which is orientation-dependent and rejects a 45° line — with the PCA eigenvalue ratio; and (c) derives the dot spacing from the metric's mass/credit algebra rather than from a fixed rule.
Legacy model projection (not a result)The prior build receipt records 15,907 emitted dots versus an owner-recorded, unreceipted family count of 37,654. The old ≈12 % threshold and ≈13 % comparator are model estimates only; neither is HOLDOUT-DTI or ORGANIZER-CONFIRMED, and this audit does not select or promote H54-A.
CostLOW — implemented, runs in seconds on CPU.
Validation statusCANNOT BE VALIDATED ON THE EXISTING PROXY The SGMC-derived proxy target is built from the same SGMC raster, so the historical PROXY-CANARY returns AUC = 0.997948 by construction. The submission's PROXY-DTI of 0.551333 is a self-consistency diagnostic only; neither number is a compliant holdout result or a score. New external data is needed to test it honestly — specifically an independent fault compilation that is not SGMC and not the catalogue.

2 · H54-B — Quaternary viability gate on state-map faults NOT RUN

LayersSGMC faults (as H54-A) intersected with: the GDR-mapped spring and well inventory already mirrored in the corpus (gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv, 27,092 rows, carrying temp_c, geotherm*_c and a dist_known_fault_px attribute), the volcanic-vent raster, and — only if the rights question is closed, see below — ComCat hypocentres.
Physical signatureYoung-expression conjunction (hypothesis). A candidate fault aligned with independently sourced thermal springs, young volcanic features, or other geologic evidence might be more likely to be recent. Such co-location is not proof of fault age or geothermal activity; the older assertion that these classes match the expert-label set was not verified.
Why it might catch catalogue-missing faultsRestricting predictions to the complement of the local catalogue mask could avoid deliberately predicting catalogued cells, but any apparent gain could also come from the way the mask and external layers were assembled. Whole-segment hide-and-recover would be required to test it.
How it differs from prior workThe corpus's H53-A applied a probe/TMI point-lineation rule and accepted 0 of 145 clusters under its frozen thresholds, failing before emission. This candidate gates per trace rather than requiring cluster alignment, which is a far weaker and more robust requirement, and it exploits the wells' own recorded distance-to-known-fault field instead of recomputing proximity.
Expected benefitPrecision, not recall: a smaller, cleaner dot set at roughly constant credit, which is exactly the direction the algebra rewards.
CostUNKNOWN / AT LEAST MEDIUM — implementation cost depends on obtaining and validating independent inputs.
Data checkThe referenced GDR rows and other inputs are not present in the current checkout. Paths/licence notes are inherited from older sibling-repository records and were not reverified here; do not assume availability or redistribution permission. ComCat rights remain unresolved and it must not be used until checked.

3 · H54-C — Catalogue topology: relay ramps and along-strike completion LEGACY IDEA; INPUT ABSENT

LayersCatalogue fault vectors only — the legacy notes cite gdr_qfaults_traces.csv. That file is not present in this checkout; no current vector input has been verified.
Physical signatureGraph and azimuth geometry. Fit the trace graph's azimuth clusters; extrapolate each trace beyond its mapped tips; find step-overs and relay zones between near-collinear adjacent traces and bridge them; project the conjugate dip direction where the basin-margin asymmetry implies an unmapped counterpart.
Why it might catch catalogue-missing faultsEn-echelon gaps or abrupt trace terminations can motivate a structural-completion hypothesis, but geometry alone does not establish an unmapped fault. Predictions outside the mapped trace reduce direct reuse of the local catalogue, yet they may still be false positives; the official label definition was not re-verified.
How it differs from prior workEarlier tip and step-over candidates (H32a, H33d, r7-nms3-dem10-scarp) emitted from raster morphology of a derived field. This one works on the vector trace graph, so geometry is exact rather than approximated by a 100 m rasterisation, and it enforces the mass budget from the algebra.
Expected benefitUnquantified; topology may suggest catalogue gaps but could also reinforce catalogue geometry. No valid performance evidence exists.
CostUNKNOWN / MEDIUM — vectors and licences would need to be obtained and checked before implementation.
Data checkThe cited sibling path GEMSDOE48/data/raw/external/gdr_qfaults_traces.csv is not present in this checkout and was not checked in the current review. Prior sibling licence notes are not sufficient to establish availability or reuse rights here.

4 · H54-D — 1 m lidar scarp morphometry BLOCKED

LayersThe 1 m DEM tiles listed in the competition's own 1m_DEM_links.csv, i.e. USGS 3DEP lidar elevation.
Physical signatureCurvature / Hessian scarp detection (hypothesis). Search for paired slope breaks or coherent profile-curvature lineaments across the high-resolution elevation surface, then test their along-strike continuity. No scarp dimensions or expected signal-to-noise are asserted without a verified study-area source.
Why it might catch catalogue-missing faultsA scarp-scale terrain signal could reveal a young surface rupture not represented in a coarser or differently compiled catalogue. The compilation history, actual tile resolution/coverage, and relationship to expert labels are unknown in this review; a terrain lineament is not by itself a fault confirmation.
How it differs from prior workCorpus DEM work used 2 m and 3 m pilots over a handful of tiles (data/pilot/dem3m/ holds two tiles). A full 1 m curvature-plus-Hough pass over the lidar-covered portion of the study area has not been run.
Expected benefitPotentially high but unquantified if suitable high-resolution tiles cover the study area; tile availability and novelty relative to the catalogue are unverified.
CostHIGH — tile downloads and roughly 1010 cells of filtering.
Data checkNOT VERIFIED / NOT VIABLE IN THIS CHECKOUT. 1m_DEM_links.csv is absent and this review did not authenticate to or fetch the competition data page. The official USGS 3DEP / The National Map landing page is linked for manual review (USGS 3DEP), but endpoint availability, coverage of the study area, licensing, and tile resolution were not checked. Therefore this is not currently a viable input path.

5 · H54-E — Bedrock / alluvium range-front contact lineaments BLOCKED

LayersA DEM (1 m preferred, 10 m workable) plus, optionally, the SGMC polygon geology to locate the contact directly.
Physical signatureContact-lineament detection. Smooth the elevation field, then detect the lineament along the bedrock/alluvium boundary from its planform sinuosity and its transverse curvature, and flag range fronts with no catalogued fault within 300 m.
Why it might catch catalogue-missing faultsA mapped or remotely measured range-front boundary could motivate an independent search for a fault trace, but not every boundary is tectonic and a boundary alone cannot establish a missing fault. The geomorphic/bedrock layer, catalogue relationship, and label definition were not verified here.
How it differs from prior workThe corpus's ctx-ridge and topo-gap-closure candidates used ridge statistics and gap statistics of a raster field. This candidate instead segments the geologic contact and characterizes its geometry.
Expected benefitModerate; largely overlapping in mechanism with H54-D but at coarser resolution.
CostHIGH and blocked on the same missing DEM list as H54-D.
Data checkBlocked identically to H54-D. Deprioritised in favour of H54-D, which subsumes it at better resolution.

What remains open

No candidate is selected, promoted, or queued for a weekly slot by this audit. All ideas remain hypotheses until trusted inputs and a complete shared whole-segment evaluator are available. The pre-existing H54-A TIFF is an archival artifact. It passes grid/dtype/in-footprint checks, but the conservative outside-footprint null/NaN check and literal lane checker fail; performance is not validated. It is downloadable for inspection only, not upload-approved.

Mechanisms here are proposals, not measured outcomes. The SGMC-derived proxy is not an admissible holdout, and this repository has no valid HOLDOUT-DTI — see Evidence and Research.

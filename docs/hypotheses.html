1 · Endpoint-tangent continuation DUPLICATE — STOP

Layers: visible catalogue-positive pixels in the local owner-mirrored data/grid/labels.tif; no new external data. Signature: estimate a local tangent at either end of a sufficiently elongated fault trace and project a short continuation under cover, excluding cells within 300 m of the visible catalogue.

Missing-fault mechanism: a mapped scarp may become inconspicuous at alluvial cover although the structure continues. Non-fault mimics: lithologic contacts, dikes/intrusions, erosional truncations, intersections, and map-sheet/digitising termini. Difference: no endpoint-tangent projector in this checkout; owner-maintained sibling projects have explored related tip/stepover ideas, so global novelty is not claimed.

Expected DTI direction: low-to-moderate recall opportunity at low mass, conditional and unquantified. Cost: low-to-medium. No holdout or score was run after the duplicate gate.

Pre-placement check: 32,491 positive surface cells; max |Spearman ρ| = 0.0585, but max surface-cell overlap within 3 px = 0.9992 vs r13_lattice_s5_00904 (threshold 0.70). Also 0.7479 vs r11_greedy_mp and 0.8853 vs r14_union_tips10_lat6. Verdict: stop.

2 · Geothermal-expression gate on mapped structures DATA CHECK REQUIRED

Layers: catalogue-excluded structures plus independently measured well/spring temperature, geochemistry, paleogeothermal deposits, and Quaternary volcanic features. Signature: require spatially coherent geothermal expression, not just the nearest isolated hot point.

Why missing faults: discharge or alteration can mark permeable structures below basin fill. Non-fault mimics: shallow mixing, irrigation, lithology, young volcanic heat, and sampling bias. Difference: combines geothermal observations with SGMC residual traces rather than geometry alone.

Expected direction/cost: possible precision gain with unknown recall loss; medium-to-high data integration cost. The official GDR INGENIOUS page lists relevant dataset packages; the binary files, exact fields, area coverage, file-level terms, and target-label lineage are not checked. Not viable for holdout testing yet.

3 · Time-series InSAR deformation SCENES NOT CHECKED

Layers: Sentinel-1 SLC time series, DEM correction, and independent geothermal context. Signature: coherent, persistent line-of-sight deformation gradients or phase discontinuities. Concealed slip/fluid-pressure change could expose a structure without a visible scarp.

Non-fault mimics: pumping/recharge, volcanic deformation, landslides, atmospheric delay, vegetation decorrelation, and processing artifacts. Difference: time-series geodesy rather than static raster thresholds. Expected direction/cost: high scientific upside but highly uncertain; very high processing cost.

Copernicus describes free/open Sentinel access, with registration for data access; the exact competition-area acquisitions were not queried. See Copernicus Data Space and NASA ASF Search. Not viable for this run.

4 · Multi-season Landsat thermal persistence SCENES NOT CHECKED

Layers: Landsat Collection 2 Level-2 surface temperature/reflectance with terrain and land-cover controls. Signature: repeatable seasonal amplitude or phase contrast after controlling for surface moisture, vegetation, albedo, and acquisition conditions. Persistent discharge could reveal buried structures.

Non-fault mimics: irrigation, wildfire, bare-rock albedo, topographic illumination, and scene artifacts. Difference: temporal persistence rather than one-date thermal proxies. Expected direction/cost: low-to-moderate, uncertain; high preprocessing cost.

USGS documents global Level-2 surface-temperature products and no-cost open data. Exact local/seasonal coverage was not checked. See USGS Landsat Collection 2. Not viable for this run.

5 · Groundwater head/discharge response STATIONS NOT CHECKED

Layers: time-stamped USGS groundwater levels/spring discharge, precipitation, and independent structure context. Signature: repeated local hydraulic response across nearby wells, aligned with a corridor after pumping and recharge controls. Blind permeable faults could connect deep and shallow aquifers.

Non-fault mimics: pumping, seasonal recharge, irrigation, basin aquifer boundaries, and well construction. Difference: temporal hydraulic response rather than static lineaments or one-time chemistry. Expected direction/cost: low-to-moderate, uncertain; high linkage/causal-attribution cost.

The official USGS Water Data site describes data/API access; exact study-area stations and histories were not queried. Not viable for this run.

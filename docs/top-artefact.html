Why the 0.2778 entry scored well, and whether we can beat it

Labels: the mechanism is VERIFIED on the registry rasters. The 0.2778 and 0.2708 values are BOARD-UNVERIFIED. No organizer receipt exists.

Short answer

0.2778 is not the top of the board. It is rank 13 on the displayed board, and the top value is 0.3774. It is the highest published score among the 15 GEMSDOE registry artefacts. Its raster is its parent (0.2708) with exactly the 2,545 dots that lie within 200 m of a USGS catalogue fault removed. Under the organizer metric, those dots can only earn credit from a new fault within 300 m, so removing them lowers the false-positive penalty. We could not show a way to beat 0.2778 with a unique file.

2. Why removing those dots raises the score

The organizer metric is DTI = TPw / (TPw + 0.2·FPw + 0.8·FNw). A false positive costs its weight (1 − max kernel), and a dot whose kernel credit is zero costs 0.2 in the denominator. Deleting such a dot leaves TP and FN unchanged, so DTI rises.

Under the round-1 scoring text, the truth is the expert set of new faults outside the USGS database (official problem page). A dot within 200 m of a catalogue fault therefore earns credit only if a new fault lies within 300 m of it. Otherwise it is a false positive. That is the mechanism to test first.

MODEL (inference, depends on unverified board values): if the removed dots earned no credit, the two board values imply a public denominator of about 20,200. That is 2,545 × 0.2 = 509 divided by (0.2778 − 0.2708) / 0.2778. It is not a score.

Caveat: the owner's audit fields are internally inconsistent (IR-54-034), and the owner manifest labels this entry UNSCORED (IR-54-035).

3. Can we beat 0.2778?

  • Not demonstrated. No file here has a board score. Our holdout measures recovery of catalogue faults, not new faults, so it cannot rank a file against 0.2778.
  • The only holdout-tied candidate (the own-model file) does not beat the holdout best, and it fails the literal lane gate. It is not a submission candidate.
  • A stricter prune (for example 300 m instead of 200 m) is the obvious next test. It would need a new holdout experiment, and this session's three-experiment budget is spent. The result would be a HOLDOUT-DTI measurement only.
  • The board's top (0.3774) is unexplained, because no receipt or method is public.

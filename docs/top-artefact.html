The mechanism (algebra, not proof)

Why deleting dots can raise the score

For unit dots, the organizer metric reduces to DTI = T / (T + 0.2·(FP − k) + 0.8·FN) when the k deleted dots earned zero credit. Deleting a dot that earns no credit lowers the false-positive mass and leaves T unchanged, so DTI rises. Deleting a dot that carried unique credit lowers T and can lower DTI. Whether the 2,545 deleted dots earned zero credit on the hidden truth is unknown.

The repository's sensitivity table (evidence/top_artefact_analysis.json, our analysis, not the owner's) shows how the implied credit T moves with the zero-credit fraction: 5,470 at 100%, 4,923 at 90%, 4,103 at 75%, 2,735 at 50%. That is a sensitivity range, not a measurement (the analysis itself says it depends on the unreceipted pair).

Answer

What we can and cannot say

Can say: a deletion-based prune can raise DTI at fixed credit, by algebra. The 0.2778 premise is not the board's top, and the GEMSDOE32 link is unverified.

Cannot say: that 0.2778 scored highest, that the prune found a new fault, or how many points it gained. We also cannot say that GEMSDOE54 can beat 0.2778, 0.3195 or 0.3774.
